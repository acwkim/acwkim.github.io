I am a postdoctoral fellow in Computer Science at Harvard University, advised by Professor Milind Tambe. I work on **LLM alignment, post-training, and AI for decision-making**. My long-term goal is to build AI systems that learn what people want and make reliable, high-quality decisions on their behalf, even when preferences are diverse, noisy, or unclear.

Toward this goal, I have worked on:

- **Multi-objective alignment:** How should AI systems trade off competing objectives? I develop methods that capture these trade-offs through compact collections of representative policies. These help developers and users explore different behaviors, choose suitable reward weights, and personalize models efficiently.
- **Reliable LLM post-training:** How can language models learn effectively from imperfect training signals? I develop methods for learning from noisy preference feedback and identifying useful synthetic interaction data for multi-turn fine-tuning.
- **AI for decision-making:** How can AI help people turn their goals into good decisions? I study how LLMs can translate natural-language goals into formal decision models. I also develop machine learning methods that accelerate the solution of optimization and control problems.

Looking ahead, I am also interested in improving LLMs for multi-turn interaction and adaptive personalization, designing reliable reward signals for non-verifiable tasks, and developing game-theoretic approaches to AI oversight.

Before joining Harvard, I completed my PhD at the MIT Operations Research Center in 2024 under the guidance of Professor Dimitris Bertsimas. I also spent the summer of 2023 as a research intern in the Machine Learning and Optimization Group at Microsoft Research. I completed my undergraduate studies at Seoul National University.
